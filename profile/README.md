## What we do

We operate two connected lines of business.

**1. A compliance platform for Digital Product Passports.** Manufacturers, importers and their
service providers use it to create, manage, validate and publish passports aligned with the
Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR), the Construction Products Regulation (CPR),
and the final-draft European harmonised horizontal standards, series **EN 18219–EN 18223**.

**2. Custom software engineering**, delivered since 2018 — web and mobile applications,
cloud-native infrastructure on AWS, data engineering, workflow automation, and the integration of
large-language-model components into business systems.

**3. AI-driven development.** We build with AI agents inside the engineering process itself, not
only as features shipped to end users — specification, implementation, review and documentation
run as agent-assisted workflows against our own codebases and knowledge bases. The Digital Product
Passport platform is developed this way. We treat it as the direction the software business is
moving in rather than an add-on service, and we are reshaping how we work around it.

## What a Digital Product Passport is

A structured, machine- and human-readable record of a product's composition, origin, environmental
footprint, repairability and end-of-life handling, reachable through a QR code or other data
carrier. Under ESPR it becomes a condition of placing a product on the EU single market.

### Platform capabilities

- **All 24 official EU languages from a single data entry** — no per-market translation work.
- **Three-tier access model** (public / professional / authority) as required by EU regulation,
  enforced through field-level classification. The public interface cannot return restricted data.
- **Industry-specific templates** maintained centrally; manufacturers are notified when a
  regulatory change means a passport needs revision.
- **Seven-year immutable audit log** with actor identification and before/after snapshots,
  covering the regulatory retention period.
- **Full data export** in open formats, meeting the portability and backup obligations of
  ESPR Articles 10(4) and 27(1)(c).
- **Static passport pages on an EU-hosted CDN**, with a permanent web address per product.
- **Web API** for integration with ERP and PIM systems.
- **GS1 Digital Link** support, for compatibility with existing barcode infrastructure.

Available under our own brand in Bulgaria, Romania, Greece, Slovakia and Poland, and white-labelled
for partners operating their own branded compliance platform.

## The regulatory timeline, stated precisely

Most published summaries conflate two different dates. The Commission's indicative timeline gives
the **adoption date of each delegated act** — not the date obligations begin. Economic operators
then receive a transition period of roughly 18 months.

| Sector | Delegated act adopted (indicative) | Obligation realistically applies |
|---|---|---|
| Iron and steel | Q4 2026 | ~2028 |
| Construction products | Q2 2027 | ~2028–29 |
| Textiles, aluminium, tyres | Q3–Q4 2027 | **~2029** |
| Furniture | 2028 | ~2029–30 |
| Mattresses, recycled content | 2029 | ~2030–31 |

**Batteries are the exception.** The digital passport becomes mandatory for EV, LMT and industrial
batteries above 2 kWh on **18 February 2027**. That date is real and fixed, because it comes from
Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 directly rather than from an ESPR delegated act — which makes it the
first hard deadline in the entire regime.

Two further facts worth knowing:

- **The EU DPP Registry has been operational since 20 July 2026.** The system every passport must
  be recorded in already exists.
- **The implementing act governing who may see which passport data** — manufacturer, market
  surveillance authority, purchaser, recycler — **missed its statutory deadline of 18 August 2026
  and has not been adopted.** The battery regime therefore begins in February 2027 with access
  tiers that are legally required and operationally undefined.

*Sources: Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, Articles 77(1) and 77(9); European Commission DG GROW,
"Guidance Document: Digital Batteries Passport — data points by category", version 2.0,
15 August 2026; the Commission's indicative DPP timeline. Figures counted from the primary
documents, not from secondary coverage.*
